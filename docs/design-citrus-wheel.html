Citrus wheel variants

Five ways to keep Citrus's circle in Tileception. They all open the same way: drag a file and hold Super. Each takes on a different weakness of a plain ring: too few slots, too much aiming, trouble at screen edges.

Every mockup below is live. Hover or tap the slices.

A · Ring

closest to Citrus

One ring of eight, with the three modes as tabs on an outer arc. Formats that need a missing helper stay in place, hatched, so the layout never shifts.

Click Convert, Tools or Send on the arc, or tap Super, to switch the ring.

How it goes

  • Hold Super mid-drag: the ring opens with the drag card in the hub.
  • Tap Super to cycle modes. The tab on the arc lights up.
  • Release on a slice. The hub shows the result and its size before you let go.

Why

  • Anyone who used Citrus can use it at once.
  • Fixed positions: WebP is always at two o'clock in Convert.

Watch out

  • Eight per mode. Video formats will not fit.

B · Sunburst

nest it

The inner ring holds groups. Hover a group and its items fan out on an outer ring, right next to it. Click a group to zoom in: it becomes the whole wheel, the way a tile becomes the whole screen.

Hover a group, then an item. Click a group to zoom in; click the hub to zoom out.

How it goes

  • Groups first: For the web, Lossless, Other, Tools, Send.
  • Moving outward over a group shows its items in the outer ring. No click needed.
  • Zooming in gives a group the full circle, for when it holds more than the outer ring can show.

Why

  • Room for everything: five groups of up to eight is forty items, two moves away.
  • Zooming matches how Tileception already moves between levels.

Watch out

  • Two rings are bigger. It needs about 260 px of screen around the pointer.

C · Fan

follows your hand

Half a wheel that opens in the direction you were dragging, so you keep moving forward instead of doubling back. Near an edge, it turns toward the middle of the screen.

How it goes

  • The fan opens across the drag's last direction of travel, centred on the pointer.
  • Six slots over 160°. Each is wider than a ring slice at the same radius.
  • A screen edge rotates it toward the centre, so it never clips.

Why

  • Shorter moves: the targets are already where your hand was going.
  • Works anywhere, including the corners where a full ring breaks.

Watch out

  • Positions depend on direction, so it builds less muscle memory than a fixed ring. Keep the order the same and only rotate it.

D · Orbit

learns with you

Round targets in orbit around the file, with gaps between them. The ones you choose most grow larger and easier to hit. Their positions never change.

Size follows use: WebP, picked 41 times, is the largest. Hover to magnify it and its neighbours.

How it goes

  • Same slots as the ring, drawn as circles on a faint orbit.
  • Hovering magnifies a target and, a little, its neighbours.
  • Use counts live with the launcher's history and decay over weeks.

Why

  • The gaps stop near-misses from landing on the neighbouring slice.
  • The wheel adapts without moving anything, so muscle memory survives.

Watch out

  • Rarely used targets get small. Keep a floor of about 28 px.

E · Flick

wildest

A wheel you stop needing to see. Direction picks the item, not distance, so the whole screen is the target. Pause and the compass appears. Once you know it, hold Super and flick upward for WebP without waiting for anything to draw.

How it goes

  • Move anywhere in the screen: the band from the hub points at the choice.
  • A pause of 300 ms shows the compass. Flicking sooner skips it, and a chip near the pointer confirms the pick.
  • The most used four sit on the cardinal directions, which are easiest to hit by feel.

Why

  • No aiming at all. A sloppy flick still lands in the right eighth.
  • Experts never wait for drawing; beginners see exactly the same layout.

Watch out

  • Eight directions is the comfortable limit for flicks. A second flick could reach a nested level, but that's hard to learn.

Build: B with D's sizing

The sunburst solves the slot limit, which is the ring's real problem once video and documents arrive. Its outer items can grow with use, as in Orbit.

Borrow: Fan's edge rule

Any of these can turn a half-wheel inward at a screen edge instead of shifting away from the pointer.

Add later: Flick

Flick is an extra way to use whichever wheel ships, not a different wheel. Keep the same directions and it comes for free.
